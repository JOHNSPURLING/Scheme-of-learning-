How do the rules of order of operations ensure calculations are interpreted correctly?

Every calculation with more than one operation is ambiguous until we agree on a rule for what to do first. BIDMAS is that agreement — follow it below.

The rule

One order, every time

Calculations must be completed in the order: Brackets, Indices, Division & Multiplication, Addition & Subtraction. Division and multiplication share equal priority and are done left to right; addition and subtraction share equal priority and are done left to right.

Why it matters

One calculation, one answer

The explanation

Calculations must be completed in the order above — brackets first, then indices, then division and multiplication (left to right), then addition and subtraction (left to right). Skipping the order, or applying multiplication before division just because "M" comes before "D" in the acronym, gives a different — wrong — result.

Why it's worth knowing

The order of operations prevents ambiguity. Without an agreed rule, the same string of numbers and symbols could be read in several different ways. Following the same rule is what lets two people — or a person and a calculator — always land on the same answer.

Worked example

Step through it: 12 − 3 × 2

Why brackets change the answer

Same numbers, different instruction

(12 − 3) × 2
Result: 6
No brackets
Add brackets

With no brackets, multiplication happens first: 3 × 2 = 6, then 12 − 6 = 6.

A calmer case

When there's nothing to argue about

12 ÷ 3 + 2 = 6

Only one priority level appears before the addition — there's no multiplication or division tug-of-war. Division is completed first regardless of direction (12 ÷ 3 = 4), then addition (4 + 2 = 6). BIDMAS doesn't need to "kick in" here because there's no competing operation of equal or higher priority to sequence.

Common misconceptions

Tap a card to check yourself

✕ Misconception

"I'll just work strictly left to right."

Tap to reveal
✓ Fix

Left to right only applies within a priority level. Higher-priority operations elsewhere in the expression still go first, e.g. 12 − 3 × 2 = 6, not 18.

✕ Misconception

"Multiplication always comes before division — M is before D."

Tap to reveal
✓ Fix

Division and multiplication share equal priority. The acronym's letter order isn't a ranking — whichever appears first, reading left to right, is done first.

✕ Misconception

"I can multiply before dealing with the index."

Tap to reveal
✓ Fix

Indices are evaluated straight after brackets — before any multiplication or division. Forgetting this step changes the value being multiplied.
