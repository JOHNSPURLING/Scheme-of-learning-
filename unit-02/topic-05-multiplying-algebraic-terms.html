Multiplying algebraic terms

How can we multiply algebraic terms accurately?

The rule

What to remember

When multiplying algebraic terms, multiply the numbers first, then the letters. For example, 2a × 3b = 6ab.

Why it matters

The bigger picture

The explanation

When multiplying algebraic terms, multiply the numbers first, then the letters. For example, 2a × 3b = 6ab. Students need to multiply coefficients, order variables alphabetically and apply index notation to repeated variables.

Why it's worth knowing

Coefficients and variable factors can be regrouped because multiplication is associative and commutative.
